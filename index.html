Using the files

Garmin direct: connect the watch by USB and copy the .FIT files into the GARMIN/Workouts folder, then eject — they appear under Training → Workouts, named with their date and week. Garmin Connect only imports completed activities, not planned workouts, so there's no upload route there. Final Surge: Workouts → add a workout to a calendar day → import/attach the .FIT, and it will sync on to a connected Garmin — but note Final Surge ignores the name stored in the file and labels every import "Run Workout", so you'll be renaming them. TrainingPeaks: drag the .FIT onto a calendar day.

All targets are written as absolute values — pace bands in m/s and heart-rate bands in bpm — so nothing depends on the threshold or zone settings of the receiving platform. Hill and stride efforts are exported as open-target steps with timed durations, because pace targets are meaningless on a gradient.

What to expect

Week one is eight one-minute runs. That is not a typo and it is not condescending — it is the amount that lets almost everyone come back on Wednesday without sore shins. The running builds from there, and by week five you'll do twenty minutes without stopping.

Go slower than feels right. Nearly everyone who struggles with this programme is running too fast. You should be able to hold a conversation. There are deliberately no pace targets here.

Repeat any week you need to. Nothing breaks if week five takes a fortnight. Finishing matters, the calendar doesn't.

At the end you run a 5K. Enter that time on the Calibrate tab and the rest of this app — pace bands, heart-rate zones, and plans for anything up to a hundred miles — opens up around it.
